Linear algebra is the study of vectors, the spaces they live in, and the linear maps between those spaces. Its ideas are few, and they reach a long way: the same theory describes the solutions of a system of equations, the geometry of lines and planes, and the behavior of a matrix acting on data.

This book develops the subject from the beginning. It starts with vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and the problem of solving $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$, then moves to the more general setting of vector spaces, where the central notions of independence, basis, and dimension take their final form.

Each section is short and meant to be read in order. Definitions and theorems are set apart from the text, proofs are given in full, and exercises close each section.
